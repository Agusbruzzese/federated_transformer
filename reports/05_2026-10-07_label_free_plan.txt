 Project: federated_transformer                Date: 2026-10-07

          all there is. Supersedes Phase 0 and tests T5/T6 of report 04; the

SUMMARY
-------
Without any ground truth, no experiment can show that the encoder recognises
vehicle CLASSES or MODELS. That claim is off the table permanently, whatever
we train. What remains possible is still substantial, provided the research
question is chosen so that labels are not needed to answer it:

  A. Federated vs central self-supervised learning (the project's own goal).
     This is a RELATIVE comparison: both models are scored with the same
     probes, so the circularity of the morphology targets does not bias it.
     This should become the main question.
  B. Is the representation physically meaningful? Use a magnetic-dipole
     simulator to create synthetic events with KNOWN speed, lateral offset,
     length and direction, inserted into real background noise. These are
     labels that do not come from our own morphology features.
  C. Does the data contain repeat vehicles? Commuters pass the gate at
     similar times on consecutive weekdays. A time-locked recurrence test can
     look for this without labels. The outcome is uncertain, and the
     experiment is cheap.

Self-supervised training itself can be improved with label-free criteria
(effective rank / RankMe, alignment-uniformity, invariance-sensitivity,
leakage), with more pre-training data (low-SNR events after a blip filter),
and with augmentation choices justified by physics. Section 3 says how, and
section 5 lists the tests.

2. THREE LABEL-FREE RESEARCH QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.A Federated vs central SSL (main question)
  Question: when events are split across clients that differ in time (days,
  runs, time-of-day slots), does federated SimCLR reach the representation
  quality of central SimCLR, and does it stay free of client identity?

  Why it works without labels: both models face the same probes, the same
  test days and the same baselines. Circular targets inflate both equally,
  so the GAP between them is still informative.

  Design:
    - Central reference: M5 SimCLR-TF recipe (report 02), 5 seeds.
    - Federated: same encoder, same augmentations, global normalisation.
      Clients = days (14), runs (4), or 6-hour slots (non-IID by traffic
      mix and noise).
    - Algorithms: FedAvg, FedProx (mu 0.01 / 0.1), local-only (no
      federation) as the lower bound.
    - Factors (one at a time): number of clients, local steps per round,
      client batch size (contrastive losses need negatives: small clients
      have few), partial participation, IID vs non-IID split.
    - Equal compute budget (total gradient steps) between central and
      federated.
  Measures:
    - proxy probes (polarity, lobes, duration) on held-out days, kNN and
      linear (report 02, section 6);
    - similarity to the central representation: linear CKA and kNN-graph
      overlap (fraction of shared 15-nearest neighbours);
    - label-free quality: effective rank (RankMe), alignment and uniformity;
    - leakage: client identity, is-0204, date, hour, noise floor, in a single
      space, calibrated with a day-level permutation null;
    - cost: rounds and bytes to reach 95 % of central quality.
  Expected contribution: a quantified central-federated gap for
  self-supervised learning on a real, non-IID sensor stream. This does not
  need vehicle labels to be a valid result.

2.B Physics-based synthetic events (closest substitute for labels)
  Idea: a passing vehicle is, to first order, a moving magnetic dipole (or a
  short chain of dipoles for long vehicles). For a single-axis sensor, the
  field over time is a known function of speed v, lateral offset d, dipole
  moment m and orientation, vehicle length L (dipole spacing) and direction.
  Simulate it, then add REAL background noise from the existing noise pool
  (ssl_central/bgpool.py) at realistic SNR.

  Steps:
    1. Implement the forward model and sample parameters over plausible
       ranges (speed at a gate after a barrier: roughly 5-40 km/h).
    2. Calibrate: fit the model to real SNR >= 30 events (least squares).
       Check that the fitted residuals are small and that the fitted
       parameter distributions look plausible (e.g. speed at night vs day).
    3. Realism check: a classifier two-sample test, real vs synthetic events
       in the encoder's embedding. Accuracy near 0.5 = realistic; near 1.0 =
       the simulator is distinguishable, and section 2.B results are weak.
    4. Probes on synthetic events: can a frozen encoder predict v, d, L and
       direction? Compare SSL vs own init vs untrained CNN vs morph8 vs raw.
    5. Fitted parameters of REAL events (step 2) are new targets that are not
       our morphology features: they come from a physical model, not from
       peak/lobe counting.
  Limits: results only transfer to the extent the simulator is realistic.
  Report the realism check alongside every result.

2.C Time-locked recurrence (looking for repeat vehicles)
  Idea: a gate is used by commuters. The same car tends to pass at a similar
  clock time on consecutive weekdays (inbound in the morning, outbound in the
  afternoon). If the embedding encodes vehicle identity, events at the SAME
  clock time on DIFFERENT days should be more similar than events at nearby
  but different times.

  Previous work (C2) found that shapes recur across runs, but a
  shuffled-shape null explained it, so no repeat vehicles were shown. C2 did
  not condition on clock time. This test does.

  Design:
    - For each weekday event, find its best match (embedding distance) among
      events on OTHER weekdays within +-10 min of the same clock time.
    - Null 1: the same search, but in a window shifted by +-60-90 min (keeps
      the time-of-day traffic mix roughly constant).
    - Null 2: weekends (few commuters).
    - Null 3: the same analysis with shuffled day labels.
    - Confound control: match on noise floor (sigma) and SNR band, so that
      shared recording conditions at the same hour cannot explain the effect.
    - Run on morph8, raw, own init and SSL.
    - Mirror test: for inbound/outbound pairs on the same day, compare the
      afternoon event with the TIME-REVERSED morning event (opposite travel
      direction). A real repeat vehicle should match its mirror better than
      chance.
  Outcomes:
    - Positive: evidence of repeat vehicles, and a natural, label-free
      re-identification benchmark (recall@k on time-locked pairs).
    - Negative: inconclusive, not proof of absence (single sensor, small
      traffic, shift workers, wall-clock accuracy +-0.35 s is not the issue).
  Pre-register the test before looking, because it is easy to over-read.

3. TRAINING BETTER WITHOUT LABELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Label-free model selection
  When there are no labels, choose checkpoints and variants with criteria
  that do not need them, and say clearly that they measure representation
  richness, not vehicle relevance:
    - RankMe / effective rank of the embedding (higher is better; predicts
      downstream performance in vision benchmarks, Garrido et al. 2023);
    - alignment (views of one event close) and uniformity (events spread),
      Wang & Isola 2020;
    - invariance-sensitivity (test T3, report 04): invariant to the
      augmentations, sensitive to sign flip and time reversal;
    - proxy probes as a regression test;
    - leakage (T8) as a hard constraint.

3.2 More pre-training data
  - Only 29 % of events are used (SNR >= 20). Build a blip filter for the
    run-0204 dropout blips (~0.2 s, 0.5-1.1k counts, near flagged dropouts),
    then pre-train on SNR >= 10 or all tiers (about 3x more events).
  - Data-scaling curve: 25 / 50 / 100 % of SNR >= 20, then + low-SNR.
    Evaluate on SNR >= 20 test days only. Watch the leakage tests: low-SNR
    data is where run identity lived before.
  - Noise-only stretches can serve as explicit negatives or as a "no event"
    class for a detection head.

3.3 Augmentations justified by physics (no task to tune them against)
  - Keep: real background noise, DC offset and drift, crop shift
    (detector jitter).
  - Time-stretch: narrow it to 0.95-1.05 or remove it. Speed is a physical
    property we may want to keep, and the stretch probably removed duration
    information from the CNN.
  - Amplitude scale: keep mild (0.8-1.25). Add log amplitude as a separate,
    globally scaled scalar input in a variant, and check leakage.
  - Forbidden as before: sign flip, time reversal.
  - Use the simulator (2.B) to check each augmentation: does it move
    the embedding of a synthetic event along the parameter it is supposed to
    represent?

3.4 Recipe
  Keep M5's recipe fixed (AdamW 3e-4, batch 256, T 0.1, 10k steps, warm-up,
  5 seeds) and change one factor at a time. Training-time gates G1-G5 from
  report 04 still apply, especially the collapse gate.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. A USE CASE THAT CAN BE VALIDATED: EVENT ANOMALY / PURITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The detector's SNR >= 20 tier still contains some non-vehicles (telegraph
  steps, pulser spikes). Plant synthetic non-vehicles (steps, spike trains,
  slow ramps, scaled to realistic amplitudes) and synthetic vehicles (2.B)
  into real noise. Measure how well a density score in the embedding (kNN
  distance) separates them: AUROC, SSL vs morph8 vs init. This gives a
  useful, label-free filtering tool whose behaviour is measurable.

5. LABEL-FREE TEST BATTERY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  From report 04 (unchanged): T1 planted-control dry run, T2 training health,
  T3 invariance and sensitivity, T4 proxy probes (regression only),
  T7 robustness by run/day/SNR, T8 leakage, T9 baselines and nulls
  (phase-randomised SSL), T10 ablations, T11 statistics, T12 federated.
  T5 (label efficiency) and T6 (same-vehicle retrieval) are replaced by:

  S1  Simulation probes (2.B): R2 for speed, offset, length; skill for
      direction; with the realism check reported.
  S2  Physical-fit targets: probes on fitted dipole parameters of real
      events (2.B step 5), held out by day.
  S3  Time-locked recurrence and mirror test (2.C), with nulls 1-3.
  S4  Representation agreement: linear CKA and kNN-overlap between seeds,
      between models trained on different runs, and between central and
      federated models.
  S5  Anomaly AUROC on planted synthetic non-vehicles (section 4).
  S6  Label-free quality: effective rank (RankMe), alignment, uniformity,
      over training and across variants.

  Statistics: resample by day then 30-min block; >= 8 held-out days for any
  final comparison; restricted Holm family; effect sizes with 95 % CIs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT THE FINAL CLAIM CAN LOOK LIKE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  "On 340 h of unlabelled single-sensor TMR data, a self-supervised encoder
  learns waveform structure that untrained networks lack, encodes physical
  passage parameters on realistic simulated events [if S1 holds], and can be
  trained federatedly across time-split clients within X of the central model
  without making clients identifiable."

  Not claimable: vehicle class or model recognition, deployment accuracy,
  generalisation to other sensors or sites.
