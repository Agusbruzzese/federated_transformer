FEDERATED vs. CENTRALIZED SPINTRONIC ENCODER — COMPARISON REPORT
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. FIRST COMPARISON — Run 4 (federated, 32-dim) vs. Centralized baseline
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SETUP
  Federated (Run 4) : 15 clients, 9 unique days (Feb 4–11 + Feb 6 time-slices),
                      20 rounds, 5 local epochs/round, latent dim = 32,
                      NT-Xent (T=0.5) + FedProx (μ=0.1), Adam LR=1e-4.
                      Best training loss: 0.2764 (still converging at round 20).
  Centralized       : same SpintronicEncoder, all 40,832 windows across 18 days,
                      100 epochs NT-Xent only (no FedProx), LR=1e-4→1e-6 cosine.
                      Best training loss: 0.2111.

QUANTITATIVE RESULTS (32-dim, 40,832 windows, 11 pseudo-label clusters)

  Metric                   Centralized    Federated    Fed retains
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Silhouette ↑                0.7965        0.7919        99.4%
  Davies-Bouldin ↓            1.8554        2.3650        78.5%
  Intra/inter ratio ↓         0.2679        0.3274        81.8%
  Training loss ↓             0.2111        0.2764        76.4%
  Downstream accuracy         95.80%        95.18%        99.4%

INTERPRETATION
  The federated model matched the centralized silhouette (−0.5%) but showed
  22–27% worse cluster compactness (Davies-Bouldin, intra/inter). On the
  downstream linear probe the gap was only 0.62 pp — geometric compression
  in UMAP did not translate to classification loss.

  Three confounds explained most of the gap:
    (a) Coverage gap: federated trained on 9 unique days, centralized on 18.
    (b) Too few rounds: Run 4 loss was still descending at round 20.
    (c) Small latent dim: 32 dims compressed fine-grained sub-cluster boundaries.

  When both models were restricted to the 8 days the federated model actually
  trained on, the intra/inter gap shrank from 22% to 17.6%. At round 30 the
  federated Davies-Bouldin (1.4766) already beat the centralized value (1.5593),
  confirming that the gap was architectural, not fundamental.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CHANGES MADE — Run 4 → Run 5
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Bug fix — drift detection
       clustering.py: .rolling(120).sum() → .rolling(120).mean()
       The sum formula amplified the drift rate by ×120, tagging 94% of
       windows as drift-affected (informational flag only, did not corrupt
       training labels).

  2. More rounds: 20 → 40
       Run 4's loss curve had not plateaued. Extending to 40 rounds gave
       the encoder time to converge past the geometric gap.

  3. Wider data coverage: 9 → 15 unique client days
       Clients 9–15 were previously time-slices of Feb 6 (redundant).
       Reassigned to 7 new full days: Feb 12, 13, 14, 18, 19, 20, 24.
       Coverage: Feb 4–24 (15 unique days).
       Also required: pipeline.py iterdir() → rglob('*.bsd') for multi-run
       directories; DATA_ROOT updated to parent dataset folder.

  4. Larger latent space: 32 → 64 dims (LATENT_DIM env var, default 64)
       Gives the encoder room to separate fine-grained sub-clusters
       (C2_s0 vs C2_s2, etc.) that were compressed in the 32-dim space.
       Pre-training init loss improved: 1.13 (32-dim) → 0.77 (64-dim).

  5. Cosine temperature annealing: T 0.5 → 0.1 over 40 rounds
       Fixed T=0.5 was appropriate early when cluster boundaries are diffuse.
       Lower temperature in later rounds pushes harder negatives and sharpens
       cluster boundaries. Server broadcasts the schedule; clients apply it.

  6. Adaptive FedProx μ: 0.20 (round 1) → 0.10 (round 40)
       Strong regularization early prevents client drift; relaxed later to
       allow local adaptation as representations stabilize.

  7. Latent re-clustering at round 12
       After 11 rounds the encoder has learned meaningful structure. At round 12
       each client recomputes its pseudo-labels directly in the 64-dim latent
       space (gap-statistic KMeans on Z) instead of the original HHT feature
       space. Labels then reflect the learned representation, not the raw features.
       Effect: single-round loss drop of 0.1346 (−48%): 0.2776 → 0.1430.

DOWNSTREAM LINEAR PROBE — PER-CLASS HIGHLIGHTS

  Federated beats centralized:
    C2_s1  EM interference:         +17.9 pp  (52.6% vs 34.7%)
    C2_s3  medium defl./mod. burst: + 8.2 pp  (43.3% vs 35.1%)

  Centralized beats federated:
    C2_s2  large defl./low IMF1:    −15.4 pp  (77.2% vs 92.6%)

  The C2_s2 deficit persists because this sub-cluster's signature varies
  across recording runs; the centralized model's access to all 18 days
  gives it cross-run coverage no single federated client holds.
  The federated advantage on EM interference and moderate-burst classes
  comes from data diversity across 15 unique days outweighing total volume.

CONCLUSION
  Run 5 closes the task-performance gap from 0.62 pp (Run 4) to 0.16 pp —
  within measurement noise for the vehicle detection application.
  The federated system retains 99.8% of centralized accuracy while keeping
  all raw sensor data private across 15 distributed clients.
