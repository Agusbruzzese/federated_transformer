SHORT ANSWER
------------
Equal in everything a fair comparison controls:
  - the same events, from one shared extraction;
  - the same model, starting weights, augmentations and loss;
  - the same amount of training data seen;
  - the same tuning effort;
  - the same evaluation;
  - decision rules fixed in advance.
Against the PRE-REGISTERED central reference (batch 256), federated is
EQUIVALENT: lobes -0.023 [-0.034, -0.012], polarity -0.010
[-0.015, -0.004]. It was confirmed on never-used events with reliable
labels (lobes -0.018, report 46).
Two things are NOT identical, and cannot be, because they are what
federated learning IS:
  - batch size, and therefore how many contrast examples each step
    sees;
  - which events can appear together in a batch.
Their effect was measured with dedicated control arms. Against the
batch-matched central variant (batch 64, the best central model seen),
federated is LOWER on lobes: -0.057 [-0.075, -0.040]. That gap is
outside the margin. It is a descriptive result, not the pre-registered
question. Polarity and duration stay equivalent. So "equivalent" holds
against the pre-registered reference, not against the best central
recipe seen.


================================================================================
PART A. THE DATA: ONE EXTRACTION, SHARED BY BOTH ARMS
================================================================================

The extraction runs ONCE, before any model exists. Central and federated
read the very same event files. Nothing in the extraction knows about
the arms.

A1. Raw signal
  - One spintronic (TMR) sensor at one site ("INL gate"), 4 recording
    runs, Feb 4-27, 2025, 340 h, 35 GB, 29 417 binary chunks.
  - Each chunk: a Values file (uint32, low 24 bits = ADC code, range
    11.19M-11.97M, no clipping) and a Time file (uint64 device ticks).
  - True sampling rate about 2416 Hz (2407-2421 per chunk), not the
    configured 2400; stored per chunk (fs_local).
  - 188 k dropouts (mostly about 105-sample bursts), concentrated in the
    run starting Feb 4 from Feb 6 onward (about 1 % of samples). Exact
    positions come from a full scan of every file.

A2. Pipeline (code: extraction/, all parameters in extraction/params.py)
  1. Decode: 24-bit codes; exact sample index rebuilt from ticks;
     chunks stitched into continuous streams per run.
  2. Detection (validated HYST-BP detector):
       - FIR 10 Hz, decimate to about 50 Hz;
       - baseline, robust sigma, high-pass;
       - hysteresis on the envelope;
       -> onset, offset, energy centroid, SNR per event.
  3. Feature path (the waveform the models see):
       - 50 Hz mains removed by regression;
       - 4th-order Butterworth low-pass 8 Hz (zero phase);
       - polyphase resample to about 50 Hz (25/1208);
       - two-pass 20 s running-median baseline SUBTRACTED;
       - dropouts of 1-4 samples filled, >= 5 split the stream and are
         masked +-0.15 s.
  4. Window: 21 s = 1 050 samples centred on the energy centroid, plus
     the onset/offset mask and the dropout/repair mask.
  5. 19 morphology scalars on the central about 8 s (m8_*): polarity,
     lobe count, duration, amplitude, ... These give the evaluation
     targets, never a model input.
  6. Selection: events with more than 20 % masked samples excluded;
     duplicates between blocks removed.
  Output: 15 260 events. Training tier: SNR >= 20 -> 4 375 events.
  Validation:
    - 33 self-tests;
    - deterministic: re-run today bit-identical, report 50;
    - morphology features at SNR >= 20 carry no date information
      (leakage skill 0.02).

A3. What the models receive (identical for both arms)
  - Input: a 12 s crop (600 samples) resampled to an exact 50 Hz grid,
    max-normalised (shape only, no amplitude).
  - Training views, the same function for both arms: random stretch
    0.85-1.18 and crop (must keep the event); amplitude x U(0.8, 1.25);
    real background noise added at U(0, 1) x the event's own noise level;
    small offset and linear drift; max-normalise.
  - Background noise comes ONLY from the event's own day (per-day pool,
    7 199 quiet segments). This is required for federated, where a site
    cannot see other days, and it is applied identically to central.
  - Evaluation view: deterministic centred crop, no augmentation.

A4. Who gets which events (3-fold cross-fitting, folds.json, frozen by hash)
  Days are assigned once, the same for both arms:
    server days  Feb 18, 20        (used by neither arm in this comparison)
    tuning day   Feb 10            (learning-rate selection only)
    fold 0  evaluation days Feb 5, 11, 25
    fold 1  evaluation days Feb 6, 13, 26
    fold 2  evaluation days Feb 7, 14, 27
  In each fold the remaining days form exactly 10 clients (1 or 2 days
  each). Fold 0 clients (SNR >= 20 events):
    434, 301, 81, 61, 324, 297, 403, 53, 366, 253  -> total 2 573
  CENTRAL trains on the union of these 10 clients (2 573 events, the same
  events). FEDERATED keeps each client's events at its site. A firewall
  asserts in code that no training event comes from an evaluation,
  tuning or (here) server day.


================================================================================
PART B. TRAINING: WHAT IS IDENTICAL, WHAT DIFFERS
================================================================================

B1. Identical (by construction, enforced in the frozen simulator fedsim3)
  item                        central C256              federated F-nat
  -------------------------   -----------------------   ----------------------
  training events             2 573 (fold 0)            the same 2 573, split
                                                        over 10 sites
  encoder                     patch transformer, 207k parameters, 64-d
                              embedding, same projection head
  initial weights             identical for the same seed (fedsim3.build)
  loss                        SimCLR NT-Xent, temperature 0.1, 30 s
                              false-negative guard
  augmentations / noise       identical function, per-day noise
  optimiser                   AdamW, weight decay 1e-4 (each site keeps its
                              own AdamW state between rounds)
  schedule shape              5 % warm-up + cosine over the same budget
                              (federated uses the central-equivalent step
                              index)
  training examples           2 560 000                 2 546 000 (99.5 %;
                                                        integer rounding of
                                                        local steps)
  passes over the data        about 5 epochs per        about 4.7-5.1 local
                              round x 200               epochs per round per
                                                        site x 200
  seeds                       0-9, all 3 folds (30 models per arm)
  tuning effort               4 learning rates each (1.5e-4, 3e-4, 6e-4,
                              1e-3), same tuning day, same frozen rule
                              ("keep 3e-4 unless another beats it by >0.02")
  monitoring / stopping       none: final weights, no early stopping

B2. Different (inherent to federated learning, each measured by a control)
  1. Batch size, and so negatives per step.
       central 256 events/batch -> 510 negatives per pair;
       federated 64 per site (53 and 61 at the two smallest sites,
       which hold only 53 and 61 events) -> 126 negatives.
       Gradient steps: central 10 000; federated 40 000 summed over
       sites (6 800 sequential).
       Why it differs: a site with 53 events cannot form batches of 256.
       Control: C64 = central with batch 64 (same examples, 40 000
       steps). Result: C64 is +0.035 above C256 on lobes. Batch size
       matters, and in central's favour at batch 64, not 256 (sec. C3).
  2. Learning rate: C256 1.5e-4, F-nat 3e-4. Each was chosen by the same
     rule with the same 4-value budget (tuning scores: C256 0.740 at
     1.5e-4; F-nat 0.713 at 3e-4). Equal effort, not an equal value: each
     arm gets its own best setting.
  3. Which events meet in a batch: in central, events from all 10 sites
     are mixed; in federated, only one site's days.
       Control: F-IID = federated with the same site sizes but events
       spread at random. F-nat vs F-IID -0.008 [-0.021, +0.005],
       EQUIVALENT. Non-IID sites cost nothing detectable.
  4. Averaging: federated averages 10 site models every round (FedAvg,
     weighted by examples, all 10 sites every round).
       Controls (ablations, report 21): resetting the optimiser each
       round, or 50 instead of 200 rounds, change nothing (both
       EQUIVALENT to F-nat).
  5. Wall time (simulator): central 933 s, federated 1 366 s per run.
     Not a quality factor.


================================================================================
PART C. EVALUATION AND RESULTS
================================================================================

C1. Evaluation (identical for every model; frozen evaluator.py)
  - The frozen 64-d embedding of each model is computed for every event.
  - Probes (kNN k = 15 and logistic or ridge regression) are fitted on
    the fold's client events and scored ONLY on the fold's 3 evaluation
    days, which no compared model ever trained on.
  - Metrics: lobe-count skill and polarity skill (chance-corrected
    balanced accuracy; primary), duration R2 (secondary); each the mean
    of kNN and linear.
  - Statistics: per arm 10 seeds x 9 days; two-stratum t (seed and day
    variation), 90 % CI.
  - Margins, fixed before training: lobes +-0.04, polarity +-0.02.
    EQUIVALENT if both CIs lie inside.
  - Nuisance gate: does the embedding encode the recording day more than
    central does? Margin 0.03.

C2. Proof that the procedure works (dry run, before federated was scored)
  - Central vs an independent set of central models: +0.003
    [-0.008, +0.014] -> EQUIVALENT. The test can say "equal".
  - Untrained network -0.324 -> INFERIOR; noised model -0.097 ->
    INFERIOR; model degraded just past the margin -> not equivalent.
  - Planted day signal -> gate FAIL. All controls passed.

  Mean scores      lobes   polarity  duration

C4. Confirmation on never-used events (reports 42, 46)
  - 5 284 events (SNR 10-20) that no model had seen; same recipe; scored
    with reliable labels.
  - F-nat vs C256: -0.018 [-0.031, -0.005] EQUIVALENT, the study's
    direction and size.
  - C64 on noisy labels was -0.039 below C256 (report 42). Batch 64's
    advantage does not hold on that tier; it was not re-measured with
    reliable labels.


================================================================================
PART D. VERDICT
================================================================================
  1. The comparison is equal in data, model, initialisation, loss,
     augmentation, noise, compute (99.5-100 % of examples), tuning effort
     and evaluation. Rules and margins were fixed before any model was
     trained, the code was frozen by sha256, the procedure was proven to
     pass and fail on planted controls, and every number was reproduced
     independently.
  2. Two properties necessarily differ (batch size per site; which
     events share a batch). They were measured, not ignored:
       - mixing of sites: no effect (F-IID);
       - batch size: central with batch 64 is the strongest central
         model. Against it, federated is about 0.057 lower on lobes,
         just outside the margin, while polarity and duration remain
         equivalent.
  3. Correct statement:
       "Federated training across 10 day-sites is equivalent to central
       training with the pre-registered batch-256 recipe (lobes -0.023,
       polarity -0.010), confirmed on never-used events (-0.018). Against
       the best central variant seen (batch 64), federated is about
       0.06 lower on lobe structure (descriptive). Server pretraining
       closes that gap: S+F is non-inferior to central (+0.026 lobes vs
       C256) and within 0.01 of C64 (0.626 vs 0.635)."
  4. What is not covered: one sensor, 14 winter days; quality measured
     with shape proxies (no vehicle labels); compute matched in training
     examples, not in time or communication.
