# External audit: research/extraction/C_detection

_20260930_021556 · served by claude-sonnet-5-5 · effort medium · 110859 in / 4027 out · ~$0.262_

## Verdict
REJECT: the headline numbers (F1 0.803 vs 0.006, clean-tier precision 0.96) rest on a strawman baseline, self-referential injection templates, one AI annotator, and a post-filter tuned on 20 samples, and the "clean tier" is a selection-biased subset. Several pre-registered criteria were dropped or changed after results were seen.

## Issues

1. [BLOCKER] REPORT §6.1 / detectors_all.det_fixed: The "legacy" baseline is a reconstruction (24-min mean DC, global MAD, k=5), not the lost code. The +0.80 ΔF1 is therefore an artefact of a strawman, and the "PROTOCOL condition met" claim is not supported. FIXED has recall 0.00 at SNR 8 and SNR90 = ∞, so the global MAD is inflated by drift, which is the reconstruction's own choice. -> Report the legacy comparison only as illustrative. Test a fairer legacy variant (fixed windows with a local baseline). FIXEDLOC already gives 0.645, so the real gain over a reasonable fixed-window method is about 0.16.

2. [BLOCKER] inject_eval / templates.py: Templates are harvested from events found by HYST (k=4) in the tuning spans, at SNR>40. Injections go into tuning-run backgrounds and holdout Run 3, so the recall of HYST-BP is evaluated on shapes its own detector selected. Injected events are high-SNR shapes scaled down, so recall at SNR 3–8 says nothing about real small vehicles, which the report itself admits. -> Add templates from independent detectors or hand-drawn shapes. Do not headline F1 and recall@8.

3. [BLOCKER] Hand labels (§6.2, all `*_labels*.csv`): The labeller is a single AI. Only V/M/F/N/U codes are stored, with no inter-rater or human check. U is 13–30 % at low SNR and is counted as not-V. The "blind" claim is weak because the sheet titles print the segment's SNR ("peak N sigma") and duration, and the post-filter is an SNR cut, so the labeller could see the very feature being filtered on. `hold` sheets also mix pass and fail. -> Have a human label at least the 50-item clean-tier sample and the holdout set with SNR hidden. Until then, downgrade every precision claim to low confidence.

4. [MAJOR] postfilter / §3.7: The SNR≥30 cut was picked from a grid of 8 SNR × 7 width values on 100 labels, with only 20 passing (18 V). The selection rule "max recall subject to precision ≥ 0.90" was invented after seeing that raw precision was 0.40 on holdout. The 0.96 holdout figure comes from 50 samples whose labelling was not blind to SNR (issue 3). "Confidence: high" is not justified. -> Downgrade to medium. Pre-register the cut and validate on a fresh, human-labelled holdout.

5. [MAJOR] §6.2 vs recommendation: Relative recall is computed against the V-labelled pool sampled *from these detectors*, so it is circular (n_V=114, drawn 25 per detector). The "clean tier keeps 59 %" figure is biased by that. The real recall of vehicles is unknown, and the clean tier is likely biased towards large, near-lane, high-SNR vehicles. This matters for the D and federated contrastive-learning training set, because the excluded 41 % and the low-SNR classes never appear in it. -> Estimate recall with independent injections into real data and with a human census of one full span.

6. [MAJOR] §6.3 plausibility: The tune-set night/peak ratio of 0.008 is a direct consequence of an SNR≥30 cut that selects only large events. The holdout value "0.000 [0,0]" is a degenerate bootstrap CI (zero night events), with no weekend data. Raw HYST-BP fails the criterion (0.24), and the final method passes only after the filter. The pre-registered criterion is not discriminative here. -> Report night event counts with Poisson CIs. State that weekend and weekday effects are untestable on the holdout.

7. [MAJOR] Holdout hygiene: Run 3 was viewed in exploration (`zoom.py`, `sanity_r3.png`) before the pre-registration was finalised. The holdout summary precision (0.40) was seen before the filter was designed. Four further holdout spans were added after seeing that the count was < 200 (`build_spans.py` comment). Surrogate design, front end, CUSUM and the hand-check design were all changed after results were seen, and FIXEDLOC and HYST-BP were added post hoc. The decision rule also ranks by a tie-break the author chose. -> List all of these as exploratory and downgrade "pre-registered" language. Confirm on unseen data (a Run 3 span not yet used, or another run).

8. [MAJOR] Operating point / surrogates: FA≤0.2/h is estimated from 24 h (tune) and 20 h (holdout) of Gaussian surrogates. At 0 events the upper bound is roughly 0.15/h, so "0 FA/h" is uninformative. The report itself shows the surrogates miss real non-Gaussian interference (hand precision at k=5 is 0.40). The "matched FA" design therefore does not control real false alarms. FIXED@k=3 and other detectors are "matched" at 0 FA/h with recall 0, which is unfair to them. The threshold grid is coarse: CUSUM's best FA is 0.65/h at holdout, failing the constraint, yet it is still compared. -> Use the real-data hand-verified FA as the operating criterion, and tune each detector on real precision.

9. [MAJOR] Detector fairness: HYST-BP has a bespoke channel (0.5 s median, 4 s high-pass, valley splitting) that was developed on the tuning data. BDET is a re-implementation on C's 50 Hz stream, with its 5 Hz LP and 60 s block sigma, not B's original chain, and it is compared at 6σ against a tuned detector. The ΔF1 vs BDET is not a like-for-like comparison. F1 treats fragments as FP, which penalises detectors that split one vehicle. -> Run B's original pipeline. Report recall and split rate separately.

10. [MAJOR] Segment length (§6.6): The 12 s window claim rests on the clean tier, which contains only large events, with a truncation threshold applied to the same clean set. `tab_durations` shows the 95th percentile of "x_dur" (ramps, ±10 s context) is ~21 s, and `truncation_ctx` at L=12 is 42 %. The "1.8 %" figure uses the hysteresis segment (down to 2σ), not the full ramp energy the brief and pre-reg asked for (1–99 % including ramps). Clean-window contamination is 12 % (22 % any event). The pre-registered rule (95th percentile of core+ramps) would give a longer L. -> Apply the rule as written, or state the deviation. Report the truncation at L=12 and L=20 with ramps included.

11. [MINOR] segment_repr.py: The contamination check counts other events from the `allev` pool only within the same block. Block edges (trimmed 30 s) and events outside the central part are missing, so contamination is underestimated at block boundaries. Also the `trunc_ctx` label is misleading.

12. [MINOR] Code, `detector.baseline_sigma`: `_block_stat` with the `lambda ... np.nanmedian` returns NaN via warnings for masked blocks. NaN is then silently replaced by the median of `s1` (a hard-coded fallback). `sigma` is also a piecewise-linear interpolation of block values, which can misplace the sigma near chunk-stitch edges. Edge effects of `median_filter(..., mode="nearest")` and `uniform_filter1d` in the HP are not evaluated (only the ±60 s context advice). -> Add an edge test.

13. [MINOR] `validate_io.csv`: `slots_equal` is False in 5 of 12 rows (missing differs by 1–13 slots). The report says "≤13 slots, <1 %" but the "agrees exactly" claim in the header conflicts with it (only Values/Time agree). The 12-chunk sample is tiny.

14. [MINOR] Stability (§6.4): The "stable" label is claimed for HYST-BP raw, but the main threshold k_on fails (+66 % / −31 %) and so does pulse_med. The clean-tier stability at SNR cut ×0.75 (+12 %, 0.895 matched) fails the pre-registered criteria, yet is described as "marginal". Fine, but the unqualified "Stable" for the whole clean tier is overstated.

15. [MINOR] Reproducibility: `run_all.sh` was not shown. The hand-labels CSVs cannot be regenerated. The `hand_candidates.csv` was overwritten and a `.labelled_backup` exists. The holdout candidate list depends on `hand_alldet.pkl`. The paths in `cio.py` are hard-coded. `postfilter make_hold` uses `random_state` only, which is fine.

16. [MINOR] Cross-workstream: B's detector is compared at 8σ with precision 0.80 (n=30, 5 U counted as not-V), which is a lower-bound and labelled once by the same AI. The claim "B's detector is a reasonable middle tier" and the use of B's 12–15 s window as agreement are not independent, because C uses B's guidance. Also, `cio.py` docstring says "Time us since run start" while A says device ticks (superseded but not corrected in the docstring).

## Brief coverage
1. Evaluation spans (night/rush/midday/evening, weekday/weekend, several runs): partial. Weekend is tune-only, spans are 20 min, and only ~7 h total over about 10 distinct days; the large-sample pass covers ~¼ of the data.
2. ≥4 detectors compared: answered (FIXED, HYST, STALTA, CUSUM, WAVELET plus variants and BDET), with fairness caveats.
3. Label-free evaluation (injection, hand-verified precision, diurnal, stability, cost): partial. All are done but each has a circularity or annotator problem (issues 2, 3, 6, 8).
4. Overlaps, drift, chunk boundaries, gaps: answered (pairs, baseline, stitching, dropouts). Overlap handling is weak below 2 s.
5. Recommend detector, parameters, segment representation, deliver `detector.py`: partial. Delivered, but the recommendation rests on the issues above, and the window rule departs from the pre-registered one.

## Required follow-ups
1. Rerun the segment-length rule as pre-registered (1–99 % energy with ramps, 95th percentile) and report L.
2. Add the correct labelling caveats: hide SNR and duration on the sheets and re-label 50–100 candidates blind, ideally by a human.
3. Compute Poisson/exact CIs for zero-count cells (surrogate FA, night events).
4. Run B's original pipeline against HYST-BP on the same holdout spans.
5. Evaluate injections with templates independent of HYST (other detector, other days or run) and at real low SNR.
6. Draw a fresh, unseen span set (other days in Run 3 or Run 2) and validate the frozen detector plus the SNR≥30 cut with human labels.
7. Quantify the clean-tier selection bias: compare amplitude, lane and time-of-day distributions of the clean vs the all-events tier, and report how much of the training set an SNR≥20 relaxation adds, with holdout validation.
8. Add the weekend and dense-dropout regimes to the holdout, or state that they were not tested.
