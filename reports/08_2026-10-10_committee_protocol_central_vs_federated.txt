SUMMARY
-------
Goal: compare, under equal conditions, three ways of training the same
self-supervised encoder (SimCLR, patch transformer, 207k parameters):
  CENTRAL      all training events in one place;
  FEDERATED    FedAvg across 10 clients (one client = one or two recording
               days), from random initial weights;
  PRETRAINED   the server first pretrains on its own days, then FedAvg
  SERVER       across the same 10 clients.

A committee of four independent members examined the central model and
designed the comparison. Each member worked from code and data, and the
orchestrator re-checked their key claims:
  A  model analyst        what the central model is, measured
  B  statistician         estimands, margins, power, decision rules
  C  federated engineer   client partition, equal-budget accounting,
                          simulator
  D  validity critic      leaks, confounds, claims each outcome allows

Main conclusions of the committee:
  1. The central model trains reliably, but the existing evaluation can
     barely separate two training recipes. Only lobe count has real
     headroom. With the 4 old test days, "federated ~ central" would be
     declared by default (power < 35 %). The protocol therefore evaluates on
     9 days that no model has seen, by 3-fold cross-fitting.
  2. Three blockers in a naive design were found and fixed:
       - the synthetic generator and the existing checkpoints were built
         from the clients' data, so they cannot be the "pretrained server";
       - the central training loop never terminates when a client has fewer
         than 256 events (7 of 13 days);
       - the shared noise pool mixes days, which is cross-client sharing.
  3. A federated simulator was built. With a single client it is
     bit-identical to the central M5 training (maximum weight difference
     0.0), so the arms differ ONLY in training topology.
  4. "Equivalent" is defined by TOST with pre-stated margins derived from
     the central model's own variability: lobes +-0.04, polarity +-0.02,
     plus a leakage gate. Each controlling factor (fewer negatives,
     averaging, non-IID clients, pretraining) has its own control arm.

Status: DRAFT. Before freezing, the protocol needs (i) an independent
review, (ii) the evaluator and fold support in the simulator, and (iii) a
dry run in which the planted controls behave as specified (section 7). The
compute budget (section 10) needs the user's approval.

2. WHAT THE CENTRAL MODEL IS (member A, measured)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(M5 SimCLR-TF, 5 seeds, 4 test days; research/model/K_committee/A_model/)

  2.1 It trains. Every seed beats its own untrained weights on every target:
      kNN lobes +0.35, polarity +0.19, duration +0.04. Effective rank
      (RankMe) rises from 4 to 25.
  2.2 Probe scores (kNN / linear, mean +- seed SD): polarity 0.880 / 0.904
      (SD 0.005); lobes 0.624 / 0.694 (SD 0.03-0.04); duration
      0.817 / 0.874 (SD 0.01).
      Day-to-day SD is larger than seed SD for lobes (0.10 vs 0.04), so
      comparisons must be paired by day.
  2.3 Noise floor: two central seeds share 70 % of their 15 nearest
      neighbours (range 0.686-0.715). CKA does not discriminate (untrained
      networks reach the same 0.85), so it is not a primary metric.
  2.4 Synthetic ground truth separates trained from untrained well (family
      skill 0.93 vs 0.74, seed SD 0.011). This is a secondary metric only;
      the generator was fitted on client days.
  2.5 Day identifiability: central TF 0.027, against a null mean of -0.016
      (q95 ~ 0), concentrated on the run-0204 days. This is the leakage
      baseline.
  2.6 Training length moves the representation more than the seed does
      (1k vs 10k steps: neighbour overlap 0.485, vs 0.699 between seeds),
      and 10k steps is not converged. Budgets must be matched exactly.
  2.7 The M5 linear lobe probe is numerically unstable (up to 0.046 off).
      Fixed in the protocol: scaling always on, converged solver.

4. PROTOCOL v1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Questions and hypotheses
  Q-F  Is federated training (F-nat) equivalent to central training (C256)?
       H0: |Delta_k| >= m_k for some primary metric k. Rejected by
       intersection-union of two one-sided tests (TOST) per metric,
       alpha = 0.05.
       Fallback reported if equivalence fails: non-inferiority
       (lower bound > -m_k).
  Q-S  Does server pretraining help federated training?
       S+F vs F-nat: one-sided superiority on the primary metrics, Holm over
       2 metrics, and point estimate >= m_k / 2.
       S+F vs C256: non-inferiority at m_k.
       "Helps federation specifically": the interaction
       (S+F - F-nat) - (S+C - C256) > 0 (secondary, lower power).
  Gate Leakage: day-identity skill of the arm minus that of C256, upper
       95 % bound < 0.03 (Welch t over seeds), measured against a
       block-permutation null.

4.2 Data (SNR >= 20 events; folds.py, folds.json)
  server days     Feb 18 + Feb 20 (run 0218), 434 events: server only.
  tuning day      Feb 10, 327 events: tuning only; never a client, never
                  evaluated.
  evaluation      the large remaining days except Feb 19 (same run as the
                  server), assigned to 3 folds round-robin within each run:
                    fold 0  Feb 5, 11, 25    1 041 events
                    fold 1  Feb 6, 13, 26    1 077 events
                    fold 2  Feb 7, 14, 27      851 events
                  -> 9 evaluation days, each scored only by models that
                  never saw it.
  clients         for each fold, all other remaining days, merged to exactly
                  10 clients. Rule: repeatedly merge the smallest client into
                  its adjacent same-run client (the smaller neighbour).
                  Training events per fold: 2 573 / 2 537 / 2 763; client
                  sizes 53-434. In every fold the weekend (Feb 8+9) is one
                  client, and Feb 4 merges with a neighbouring run-0 day.
  noise           per-day pools (C: data/k_committee/C_federated/
                  bgpool_day.npy, 5 014 segments). Feb 6, 8, 9 and 11 use the
                  documented relaxed rule. Every event draws noise only from
                  its own day, in every arm. Code asserts that the raw
                  bgpool.npy is never loaded.

4.3 Arms (per fold; every arm trains on the SAME client events)
  arm     description                               budget (examples)
  ------  ----------------------------------------  -------------------------
  C256    central, batch 256 (M5 recipe)            X = 2 560 000
  C64     central, batch 64 (negatives control)     X
  F-IID   FedAvg, 10 IID clients, same sizes        X
  F-nat   FedAvg, 10 natural clients                X
  S+F     server pretrain (P = 512 000 on server    P + X
          days), then F-nat
  S+C     same server checkpoint, then C256         P + X
  F-X+P   F-nat with budget X + P, random init      P + X (equal total)

  Shared by all arms: architecture; initial weights per seed
  (torch.manual_seed(seed)); M5 augmentation chain with own-day noise;
  NT-Xent T = 0.1 with 30 s guard; AdamW (wd 1e-4); 5 % warm-up + cosine on
  the arm's own budget; final weights (no early stopping, no restarts).
  Federated: R = 200 rounds, all 10 clients every round, local batch
  b_k = min(64, n_k), local steps chosen for equal local epochs (each event
  seen ~1 160 times in every arm), FedAvg weighted by examples, persistent
  client AdamW moments. Ablations (descriptive): moments reset per round,
  R = 50.
  Server pretraining: one checkpoint per seed, shared across folds and
  across S+F / S+C; hash-pinned.

4.4 Equal tuning budget
  Peak learning rate in {1.5e-4, 3e-4, 6e-4, 1e-3}, chosen separately for
  C256, F-nat and S+F, on seed 0, fold 0 clients, scored on Feb 10. The
  selection score is the mean of the primary metrics (section 4.5) and is
  fixed now. C64 and S+C inherit the C256 choice; F-IID and F-X+P inherit
  the F-nat choice. Ties go to 3e-4 (the M5 value).

4.5 Evaluation (identical evaluator for every arm; outside the FL system)
  - Embed the evaluation-day events and the fold's client events with the
    arm's encoder (eval view: centred 12 s crop, max-normalised).
  - Probes are fitted on the client events and scored per evaluation day:
      kNN       k = 15, StandardScaler on;
      linear    LogisticRegression(C = 1, float64, tol 1e-8, converged) /
                RidgeCV for duration, StandardScaler on.
  - Targets: polarity, lobe count (capped at 4), log10 duration
    (morph-derived, circular: valid for RELATIVE comparisons only).
  - PRIMARY: lobes and polarity, each as the mean of kNN and linear skill.
  - SECONDARY (90 % CIs, no claims):
      duration;
      per-probe scores;
      15-NN overlap with central (noise floor ~0.70, re-measured);
      gap closure G = (arm - init) / (C256 - init);
      synthetic family / stretch probes (generator fitted on client days:
        descriptive only);
      RankMe (not a quality metric), alignment, uniformity, CKA;
      the decomposition C256 -> C64 -> F-IID -> F-nat.
  - LEAKAGE: day-identity skill on the evaluation days (logistic,
    GroupKFold by 30-min blocks), with a block-permutation null and a
    version conditioned on morph8, hour and has_dropout.

4.6 Statistics (member B)
  - Score Y[arm, seed, day]. Delta_k = mean over evaluation days of
    (arm mean - C256 mean) for that day. Days are nested in folds; fold
    effects cancel within the day pairing.
  - Model: Y = mu_arm + u_day + v_arm,day + w_arm,seed + e. Two-stratum
    t-procedure, SE^2 = S_seed (1/n_A + 1/n_C) + S_day / D, Satterthwaite
    df. Seed-paired analysis as a sensitivity check.
  - Margins (from central variability; each <= 1/4 of what SSL learns over
    its own init):
      lobes     m = 0.04   (12 % of the SSL gain 0.33)
      polarity  m = 0.02   (14 % of the gain 0.14)
      duration  m = 0.03   (secondary; non-monotone in training)
      leakage   m = 0.03   (SimCLR-CNN, a known leakier recipe, is +0.056)
  - Power with 5 seeds and 9 days, interpolated from B's simulation
    (section 5; 0.70 at 8 days, 0.93 at 13): lobes about 0.75 at an
    arm x day interaction of 0.03; polarity >= 0.97. Type I error <= 0.06
    in every simulated cell. To be re-simulated for D = 9 before freezing.
  - Decision table (per contrast vs C256):
      both 90 % CIs inside +-m, leakage OK      -> EQUIVALENT
      both lower bounds > -m, leakage OK        -> NON-INFERIOR
      an upper bound < -m                       -> INFERIOR on that metric
      a lower bound < -m, upper bound > -m      -> INCONCLUSIVE ("not
                                                   shown", never "equal")
      primaries pass, leakage upper >= 0.03     -> equivalent/non-inferior
                                                   BUT LEAKIER
      leakage lower bound > 0                   -> LEAKIER (positive
                                                   evidence)

4.7 Controls and dry run (must behave as stated BEFORE the real arms are
    scored)
  C1  central vs central: C256 seeds 0-4 vs C256 seeds 5-9 (extra runs)
      -> must be EQUIVALENT on both primaries.
      (On old data with 3 vs 2 seeds and 5 days, lobes was "not shown":
      CI -0.050..0.027. The procedure needs the full design.)
  C3  untrained init vs C256 -> must be INFERIOR (observed -0.33 / -0.14).
  C4  C256 embeddings + Gaussian noise tau = 0.5 SD -> must FAIL
      (observed lobes -0.13).
  L2  planted per-client offset in the evaluation embedding, at the
      smallest strength the permutation null detects -> leakage gate must
      flag it. The weak grid (kappa 0.005-0.05) must be run first; B only
      ran a saturating strength.
  If any control misbehaves, the procedure is fixed and the dry run
  repeated before the real arms are scored. Every dry-run output is
  logged with the protocol hash.

4.8 Monitoring and reporting
  - Logged per round (C's simulator): client losses, update norms, drift
    ratio, held-out NT-Xent on Feb 10, effective rank.
  - Collapse and divergence are flagged and reported, never acted on.
  - All seeds and all arms are reported, including failures.
  - Results are given per day, per fold and pooled.

4.9 Claims allowed (member D, fixed in advance)
  - F-nat equivalent      "Within +-m on these morph-derived proxies, on 9
                          unseen days." NOT "federated is as good as
                          central"; nothing about vehicles; no
                          generalisation to other partitions or sensors.
  - F-nat inferior        "This FL recipe, under this partition, loses X."
                          Attributed to non-IID only if F-IID closes the
                          gap; to negatives only if C64 reproduces it.
  - F-nat better          treated as suspect first (leakage, tuning
                          asymmetry, central over-training).
  - S+F helps             "Pretraining on disjoint server days helps", only
                          if it also beats F-X+P (equal total compute).
  - Leakage differences   only against the calibrated null, conditioned on
                          dropout and hour.
  - Everything            exploratory; SNR >= 20 only; one sensor, one site.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. DISAGREEMENTS BETWEEN MEMBERS AND HOW THEY WERE RESOLVED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Evaluation data. C designed for the 4 old test days (P11); B showed
    that 4 days give < 35 % power for lobes and recommended cross-fitting.
    Resolved: cross-fitting over 9 days, keeping C's server run and
    equal-budget rules.
  - Seeds. B recommended 10 seeds; B's own power table shows days, not
    seeds, are binding. Resolved: 5 seeds per fold (15 seed-fold units per
    arm), plus 5 extra central seeds for control C1. Option for 10 in
    section 10.
  - Tuning. C proposed a 4-configuration grid per arm on Feb 10; D required
    an equal budget. Resolved: the same 4 learning rates for the three
    "parent" arms, inherited by their controls (section 4.4).
  - Metrics. A recommended kNN only (unstable linear probe); B fixed the
    linear probe (scaling on, converged, change <= 0.003). Resolved: B's
    composite (kNN + linear).
  - Duration. A: moderate sensitivity. B: gain 0.053, non-monotone,
    infeasible margin. Resolved: secondary.
