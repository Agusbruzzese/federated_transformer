4. INTERPRETATION (what changed, and what it does not prove)
  a. Federated vs central holds on new vehicles, in the favourable
     direction, and with clearly less day-specific nuisance in the
     embedding. This is the study's central result, and it is confirmed.
  b. Server pretraining helped on clean events (+0.049) but not on noisy
     ones. Two explanations remain, and this test cannot separate them:
       (i)  the server's own data are now noisy too (477 low-SNR events),
            so pretraining learns less;
       (ii) the S+F learning rate (1e-3) was tuned on the clean tier and
            is not re-tuned here (by design).
     The deployed recipe S+F is still safe: it is EQUIVALENT to central
     and to plain federated on these events. Its ADVANTAGE is
     tier-dependent.
  c. The batch-64 caveat reverses, so the study's "fewer negatives help"
     observation is tier-specific. It stays descriptive, as it always was.
  d. Limits:
       - same days, same sensor (not a new season or site);
       - the margins were derived on the clean tier;
       - low-SNR probe labels are noisier;
       - SecAgg+ was tested on fold 0 only.

5. CONSEQUENCES FOR THE PROJECT
  - Federated deployment: confirmed on unseen events. Keep it.
  - Secure aggregation: confirmed harmless. Keep it.
  - Server pretraining: keep it (never worse), but stop claiming a gain in
    general. Report 40's bottom-line point 2 must be read with this
    restriction: the gain is shown for SNR >= 20 events only.
